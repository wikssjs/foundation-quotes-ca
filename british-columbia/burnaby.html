Basement waterproofing in Burnaby usually comes down to choosing whether to tackle water at the source (exterior excavation) or manage water after it enters (interior drainage). Burnaby’s housing stock plays a major role: with 35.5% of homes built before 1981, many properties are more likely to have aged membrane details and failing weeping tile, which can lead to recurring seepage even after smaller patch jobs. At the same time, Burnaby’s Lower Mainland–Southwest conditions—high groundwater periods, frequent heavy rainfall, and persistent saturation—can keep hydrostatic pressure against basement walls long enough that “dry” seasons don’t solve the underlying problem.

Cost is also shaped by local access and labour realities. When excavation hits rocky sections, mechanical breaking may be required, pushing labour higher. Contractor availability can also affect schedules and pricing during peak spring demand, because foundation drainage work tends to cluster with dry-weather excavation windows.

Permits & regulations in British Columbia

In British Columbia, foundation excavation, structural crack repair, and changes to lot drainage typically require a building permit. If the scope includes connecting a sump discharge line to a municipal sewer system (storm or sanitary), municipal approval is often required before work proceeds. For structural crack repair—particularly horizontal cracks in block walls or major step cracks—a structural engineer’s assessment is frequently needed to confirm whether the foundation is stable or whether underpinning or other structural measures are required.

Here’s a practical way for a homeowner in Burnaby to verify a contractor before hiring: (1) Check the contractor licence status using the appropriate online provincial registry—match the legal company name to the quote and contract. (2) Ask for a current certificate of insurance showing general liability and indicate the project address (or confirm coverage terms). (3) Confirm WSIB/WCB coverage (or the applicable clearance letter/confirmation) to ensure the crew is covered while on site.

For structural scopes, ask whether the contractor can provide engineering support documentation and how they handle engineer sign-off. Finally, request a written scope that clearly states what permits are included, who pulls them, and what inspections are expected. In a region where many basements involve older foundation systems—Burnaby has a large homeowner base and 249,125 residents in 2021 (Statistics Canada, 2021 Census)—it’s common for paperwork and documentation to be part of doing waterproofing correctly, not an afterthought.

Interior vs exterior waterproofing — what does Burnaby need?

The key difference between exterior and interior waterproofing is what it does to the water at the foundation interface. Exterior waterproofing—full excavation, new membrane, new drainage tile, and properly managed backfill—addresses the source of water entry and relieves hydrostatic pressure before water reaches your basement walls. It’s usually the most permanent fix, but it costs more and involves major landscaping disruption. Interior waterproofing—such as a perimeter drain channel, sump pit, and sump pump—captures water after it enters. It can be less invasive and often cheaper, but it does not stop pressure on the wall; it manages the outcome and requires good pump reliability.

Burnaby’s wet, mild winters and frequent freeze-thaw cycles mean small cracks and joints can keep opening and letting water track behind coatings. For poured concrete walls, crack injection is often an appropriate component because the wall tends to have more straightforward sealing planes; where the exterior system is compromised, exterior work still tends to be the best long-term solution. For block foundations, interior drainage is frequently a practical complement because moisture can migrate through porous areas and through joints.

Sump pump backup matters in British Columbia. During spring flooding and heavy rain, power interruptions can occur, and pump downtime can mean rapid basement flooding before backup engages. If you’re choosing interior drainage, insist on details for a battery or water-powered backup strategy.

Example: if you’re facing recurring seepage along multiple exterior wall segments in Metrotown and inspection suggests failed perimeter drainage, investing toward the exterior band (often $15,000–$30,000) can be justified because interior-only measures may require ongoing maintenance and pump dependency. If the issue is localized and you’re primarily seeing dampness near a single wall, interior perimeter drainage in the $8,000–$18,000 range may solve the problem without the need for full excavation.

How to choose a waterproofing contractor in Burnaby

Choosing a waterproofing contractor in Burnaby should start with documentation and clarity, not just the lowest line price. In British Columbia, confirm the contractor’s licence details (and that they match the legal name on your contract). Ask for a certificate of liability insurance—ensure it covers the project and is current. Then verify WSIB/WCB coverage: you want proof the workers are properly covered; many firms provide a clearance letter or confirmation documentation. If a contractor hesitates or can’t provide these items quickly, that’s a warning sign for a scope involving excavation and concrete work.

Next, request 2–3 itemised written quotes rather than one lump-sum. Look for a breakdown of labour and materials (membrane type, drainage pipe specifications, pump model, discharge method, concrete cutting/restoration, disposal, and reinstatement). Pay close attention to exclusions: is mould remediation included if efflorescence is present? Is the permit pull included (when needed)? Is excavation disposal and dump fees included, or added later as a change order?

Warranty is equally important. Ask for a workmanship warranty length (and what qualifies as a claim), the product/manufacturer warranty details, and whether warranties are transferable if you sell the home. For payment schedule, a common safe approach is to keep upfront deposits modest—never more than about 10–15%—and hold back the remainder until the work is complete and inspected. Finally, get the timeline and key milestones in writing: start date, estimated duration, and when exterior backfill/finishing will happen.

Frequently asked questions — waterproofing in Burnaby

How long does waterproofing last?

In Burnaby and the Lower Mainland–Southwest, lifespan depends on whether the fix addresses the source of water entry. Exterior membrane systems paired with replaced drainage tile commonly last 15–25+ years when installed with proper backfill, sealed connections, and functioning discharge. Interior drainage (perimeter drain channel plus sump) typically manages seepage for 10–20 years, but it relies on pump reliability, correct float control, and an adequately sized sump pit. Crack injection can last 5–15+ years, yet it’s best viewed as a component—if the perimeter drainage fails or hydrostatic pressure remains high, new leakage can appear at adjacent joints. Because 35.5% of Burnaby homes were built before 1981 (Statistics Canada, 2021 Census), older foundations often need broader systems work rather than repeated patching.

Can I waterproof my basement from the inside only?

Yes, you can waterproof from the inside only in many Burnaby basements, especially when excavation is difficult due to landscaping, decks, or finished basements. Interior perimeter drainage (French drain channel) plus a sump pump can significantly reduce seepage and help prevent standing water. However, interior-only methods don’t remove the ongoing hydrostatic pressure against walls; they’re more effective when the exterior waterproofing isn’t the sole failure point. If your weeping tile is failed or disconnected, interior-only work may become a long-term maintenance and pump-dependency situation—especially during prolonged coastal rainfall. For pricing, interior systems typically fall around $8,000–$18,000, while exterior excavation is often $15,000–$30,000 when the source must be rebuilt.

What causes foundation cracks in Burnaby?

Foundation cracks in Burnaby are commonly tied to moisture movement, seasonal freeze-thaw, and settlement influenced by drainage performance. In the Lower Mainland–Southwest, persistent saturation can increase water pressure against basement walls; when water penetrates and later freezes, freeze-thaw can widen cracks and joints. Older homes—35.5% built before 1981 (Statistics Canada, 2021 Census)—are also more likely to have aging drainage details that no longer manage groundwater effectively. Structural issues can still occur, but many “leaking cracks” are primarily about water path and pressure rather than major structural collapse. To choose the right repair, contractors should identify whether cracks are hairline versus structural/horizontal and determine whether the crack is actively leaking. That’s what guides whether polyurethane or epoxy injection is appropriate.

How do I compare waterproofing quotes?

Comparing quotes in Burnaby is easiest when you line them up by scope, not by total price. Ask each contractor for an itemised breakdown: excavation extent (if exterior), drainage tile route and pipe specifications, membrane type, pump model (including backup), sump discharge method, crack injection type, and whether mould/efflorescence remediation is included. Confirm what’s excluded—permit pulling, disposal fees, landscaping reinstatement, and any engineering requirements for structural crack assessment. For timing and accountability, check if the quote includes a start date, estimated duration, and specific warranty terms. Finally, verify credentials: in British Columbia you should see licence information, liability insurance and WSIB/WCB coverage. Quotes can differ by 30–50% because labour and access constraints vary, especially on tight Burnaby lots.

How long does basement waterproofing take in Burnaby?

Typical timelines in Burnaby range from a few days to several weeks depending on whether work is interior-only or requires exterior excavation. Interior drain channel and sump installations often take about 1–2 weeks including concrete cutting, plumbing, and finishing time, assuming finishing is limited or planned. Crack injection can be faster—often within several days once access is prepared and any remediation is complete. Full exterior excavation + membrane work usually takes longer, commonly 2–4+ weeks, because excavation, mechanical breaking (if needed), inspection, membrane/detailed sealing, drainage installation, backfill, and landscape reinstatement all require time and sequencing. Rainy conditions and freeze-thaw planning matter in British Columbia, so the best contractors schedule critical waterproofing steps around workable weather windows.

What is a weeping tile and does my Burnaby home have one?

A weeping tile is a perimeter drainage system around your foundation that collects groundwater and directs it to a sump or discharge point. In many older Burnaby homes, it was installed to relieve hydrostatic pressure, but it can fail due to age, disconnection, collapse, or clogged connections—especially if original grades and discharge paths changed over time. Given that 35.5% of Burnaby homes were built before 1981 (Statistics Canada, 2021 Census), the likelihood of an older tile system exists, but you can’t assume it’s functioning. The only reliable way to know is a site inspection: contractors may camera-inspect accessible sections, verify sump presence and discharge routing, and look for signs like sediment, disconnected pipe runs, or staining patterns matching the perimeter. If your home is damp after heavy rain, the weeping tile may be compromised even if it originally existed.
