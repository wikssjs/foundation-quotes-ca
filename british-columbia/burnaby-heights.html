Permits & regulations in British Columbia

In British Columbia, foundation excavation, structural crack repair, and changes to lot drainage commonly require a building permit. If you’re doing more than a cosmetic crack patch—particularly for major horizontal cracks in block walls, correcting subsurface drainage flow, or connecting/altering discharge routes—plan on permitting or at least an informed check with the local authority before work starts. Sump pump installations that tie into storm or sanitary sewer systems generally need municipal approval, because discharge locations and routing must be controlled to prevent nuisance or backflow.

For structural crack repair, it’s also common to require a structural engineer’s assessment. The goal is to confirm whether the wall is moving and whether underpinning or structural reinforcement is required before any sealing. A good waterproofing contractor in Burnaby Heights will tell you up front if engineering input is likely and will coordinate it rather than treating all cracks as “sealable.”

To verify contractor credibility step-by-step, start by checking the contractor’s licensing/registration status through the appropriate BC online registry for the type of work they claim to do. Next, ask for a certificate of insurance (liability) that lists you as an additional insured where applicable. Then request confirmation of WSIB/WCB coverage (or the equivalent clearance documentation used in BC). Finally, for structural-scope jobs, confirm whether they have engineering support and whether they can provide documentation for the permit submission package.

Interior vs exterior waterproofing — what does Burnaby Heights need?

The key difference in Burnaby Heights is whether you stop water before it reaches the foundation or you intercept it after it enters. Exterior waterproofing involves full excavation, installing a waterproofing membrane, reworking perimeter drainage (new weeping tile), and then backfilling. Done correctly, it permanently reduces the hydrostatic pressure against the wall, but it’s disruptive and can be expensive in tight urban lots.

Interior waterproofing—like a perimeter drain channel, sump pit, and sump pump—doesn’t stop the soil water pressure from reaching the wall; instead, it captures water that makes it into the basement and keeps floors and walls drier. In the Lower Mainland–Southwest, where groundwater saturation and prolonged rainfall are frequent, this can be a practical solution—especially when excavation access is limited or when homeowners want to protect landscaping and hardscape. Poured concrete foundations often respond well to targeted crack injection where cracks are stable, while block foundations often benefit from interior drainage as a complement because water can track through joints and small voids.

British Columbia’s winter wet season and potential spring storm impacts also make sump pump backup systems a smart safety measure. A battery backup can keep the system running if power fails, reducing the risk of overflow during a heavy rain event.

For example, if your issue is concentrated at a short, stable crack line in a poured-concrete wall, injection can sit near the foundation crack repair band of $500–$2,000, sometimes combined with targeted drainage fixes. But if your perimeter weeping tile is failing and you’re seeing recurring dampness after prolonged rainfall, exterior waterproofing—typically $15,000–$30,000—may be justified because it rebuilds the perimeter drainage system rather than repeatedly managing symptoms indoors.

How to choose a waterproofing contractor in Burnaby Heights

Choosing a waterproofing contractor in Burnaby Heights is about verifying credentials and matching the proposed method to the water source. Start by confirming British Columbia licensing/registration for the type of work they’re doing, then verify liability insurance—ask for the certificate and confirm coverage limits are appropriate for foundation excavation and interior demolition. Next, check WSIB/WCB coverage: request documentation and ensure it’s valid for the period of your project. If a contractor can’t provide these items promptly, treat it as a major risk.

Get 2–3 itemised written quotes that break down labour and materials, not a single “lump sum.” Ask whether the scope includes excavation support and disposal for exterior work, drilling/coring for interior systems, and whether permits are included or billed separately. For structural crack repairs, ask whether they anticipate an engineering review and how they’ll coordinate it. Clarify what’s excluded: for example, drywall replacement, concrete patching beyond the injection area, or mould remediation if efflorescence is present.

Warranty matters in this climate. Request the workmanship warranty length and whether it covers both the installation and the system’s function (drainage path, sump performance, discharge). Also ask for the manufacturer’s product warranty and whether it’s transferable to future owners. For payment schedule, don’t pay more than 10–15% upfront; hold back a portion until the work is complete and cleanup is confirmed. Finally, get a start date and completion timeline in writing—waterproofing schedules often depend on drying/curing windows in the Lower Mainland’s winter wet season.

How long does waterproofing last?

What causes foundation cracks in Burnaby Heights?

Foundation cracks in Burnaby Heights are usually caused by a mix of aging materials, water pressure, and seasonal temperature changes. If you suspect structural movement, don’t rely on generic sealing; ask for the right crack evaluation and engineering input where needed, then match the repair method (epoxy vs polyurethane) to whether the crack is active.
