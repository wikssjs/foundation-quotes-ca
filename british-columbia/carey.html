Permits & regulations in British Columbia

In British Columbia, some foundation-related work and changes to drainage typically require permits, especially when excavation affects structural components or when you’re altering how water leaves the property. In Carey, foundation excavation, structural crack repair (depending on severity and whether structural stability is affected), and changes to lot drainage usually require a building permit. When a contractor proposes structural-level solutions, ask whether they include engineering support and whether their team works with qualified professionals as required.

Interior vs exterior waterproofing — what does Carey need?

The fundamental difference is simple: exterior waterproofing prevents water from entering; interior waterproofing manages water once it gets in. Full exterior excavation—membrane installation, new drainage tile, and careful backfill and re-grading—targets the source and typically offers the most complete control in wet Lower Mainland–Southwest conditions. However, it’s disruptive and is more expensive because it involves digging around the entire foundation, dealing with soil saturation, and restoring landscaping and hardscapes.

Interior systems—like an interior French drain channel, perimeter drain, sump pit, and sump pump—capture seepage at the footing line and relieve hydrostatic accumulation. They’re less invasive and often make sense when the exterior is impractical due to access, landscaping, or hardscape. The trade-off is that interior retrofits don’t stop hydrostatic pressure from acting on the wall; they control the water load, which is why proper pump sizing and discharge routing are crucial.

In Carey’s typical conditions, poured concrete walls often respond well when cracks are properly assessed and injected, especially when paired with drainage control. Block foundations can benefit greatly from interior drainage when exterior options are limited, since block systems can allow moisture migration through joints. And because British Columbia spring weather can drive short-term high water volumes, backup sump systems are a serious consideration—if power goes out while rainfall peaks, your basement can still flood quickly.

A practical pricing example: if you’re dealing with localized seepage along one section of footing and there’s no evidence the perimeter drainage is failing, crack injection plus targeted interior drainage may stay closer to the $500–$2,000 crack repair band and the $8,000–$18,000 interior drainage band. But if the entire perimeter shows recurring moisture after heavy rain, exterior excavation is often justified even when it reaches $15,000–$30,000 because it addresses the source outside the foundation—where the water pressure actually builds.

How to choose a waterproofing contractor in Carey

Start with proof. In British Columbia, verify the contractor’s applicable licence status for the scope being offered, then request a current certificate of liability insurance that lists your property as an interested party where possible. Ask for WSIB/WCB clearance or coverage documentation and confirm the dates match your project window. If the contractor is doing work that touches structural stability—like horizontal crack repairs—ask whether they coordinate engineer involvement for your specific case.

Next, get 2–3 itemised written quotes that separate labour and materials (membrane, drainage tile, pumps, check valves, excavation/disposal, and interior finishing allowances). A lump-sum quote makes it hard to compare apples-to-apples scope. Read the exclusions line-by-line: is permit pulling included? Is disposal included for excavated soil? Are restoration items (backfill compaction, re-seeding, or re-laying paving) part of the scope, or treated as extras?

Warranty should be clear and in writing. Confirm the workmanship warranty length, whether there’s a product/manufacturer warranty for membrane or injection materials, and whether the warranty is transferable to future owners. For payment terms, never agree to large upfront payments; keep deposits around 10–15% and hold back final payment until the job is complete and deficiencies are corrected. Finally, insist on a timeline in writing with a realistic start date and completion estimate that accounts for inspections, curing/drying time for injections, and re-finishing.

Frequently asked questions — waterproofing in Carey

What causes foundation cracks in Carey?

In Carey and across the Lower Mainland–Southwest, foundation cracks are commonly triggered by a combination of moisture pressure and freeze-thaw. When groundwater or prolonged rainfall saturates backfill, hydrostatic pressure can push water through construction joints and small pathways, and seasonal freeze-thaw widens those openings over time. In addition, if the perimeter drain (weeping tile) is failing or undersized, water can stay against the foundation longer, increasing the likelihood of seepage that eventually becomes visible cracking. Homes with older foundations are also more prone to compromised drainage performance. If cracks are horizontal or “step-like” and show signs of movement, the cause may be structural and should be evaluated before sealing or injection.

How do I compare waterproofing quotes?

To compare waterproofing quotes in Carey, ask for an itemised scope that clearly separates excavation, drainage tile work, membrane materials, interior drain components, sump equipment, and disposal/restoration. Look for details like whether they’re replacing failing weeping tile versus “repairing” it without confirming slope and discharge. Make sure the quote specifies the type of crack injection (epoxy for structural stabilization vs polyurethane for active seepage) and whether they’re addressing the water source through exterior drainage or only managing symptoms with an interior perimeter drain. Pricing alone can mislead—an exterior quote can land in the $15,000–$30,000 band while an interior system might be $8,000–$18,000, depending on access and how much of the perimeter must be rebuilt.

How long does basement waterproofing take in Carey?

Timelines in Carey depend heavily on whether the work is exterior excavation or an interior retrofit. For interior perimeter drainage and a sump system, many projects can progress in stages over about several days to a couple of weeks, depending on drying/curing time, pump install scheduling, and how much interior demolition and re-finishing is needed. Exterior waterproofing typically takes longer because of excavation, membrane application, installation and inspection of drainage tile, backfill compaction, and then landscape/hardscape restoration. Freeze-thaw conditions and rainy stretches can also affect excavation scheduling and drying times. Your contractor should provide a written start date and completion estimate, including allowances for inspections and curing for injections (especially if active leaks are being controlled).

What is a weeping tile and does my Carey home have one?

A weeping tile is a perimeter drainage system installed around (or near) the foundation to collect groundwater and direct it away from basement walls and the footing area. In many older Greater Vancouver-area homes, the original weeping tile was installed to manage seepage, but after decades it can clog, lose slope, or fail completely—leading to persistent moisture and higher pump run times. Whether your Carey home has one depends on the age of the basement build and what was installed originally. Some homeowners discover the system during excavation for exterior work or by tracing lines near the perimeter. If you’re seeing water at the footing line after heavy rain, it’s a strong sign that your drainage system may be undersized or failing and should be inspected before you choose between interior drainage ($8,000–$18,000) and a source-focused exterior approach.

Can I waterproof my basement in winter in Carey?

In many cases, you can start certain waterproofing work in winter in Carey, but it depends on ground conditions and the type of project. Interior waterproofing—like sump pit work, interior perimeter drains, and crack injection—can often proceed when the basement interior is accessible and surfaces can be treated properly. However, exterior excavation is more challenging during freeze-thaw and cold snaps because saturated ground and frozen sections slow excavation and can affect backfill compaction and membrane detailing. Rain can also delay exterior work. A contractor should assess whether soils can be excavated safely, whether drainage slopes can be properly built, and whether any curing/drying requirements can be met. If your basement has active leaks, it’s often possible to stabilize conditions first, then complete exterior source control when conditions improve.

What is the difference between waterproofing and damp-proofing?

In practical Canadian use, “damp-proofing” generally refers to measures intended to reduce moisture penetration or manage minor dampness, while “waterproofing” is intended to handle hydrostatic pressure and active water entry during wet periods. In the Lower Mainland–Southwest climate, that distinction matters because prolonged rainfall and groundwater saturation can keep pressure against basement walls and slabs. Damp-proofing products may help with light condensation or small seeping areas, but they often don’t provide the drainage control needed for true leakage. For Carey homeowners with repeated seepage after storms, integrated solutions—like an interior drain and sump system in the $8,000–$18,000 range or, when needed, exterior excavation and membrane work in the $15,000–$30,000 range—tend to be more appropriate. Local contractor scope and the exact water entry points determine which level of protection you actually need.
