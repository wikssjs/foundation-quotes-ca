Permits & regulations in British Columbia

In British Columbia, foundation-related work often falls under the “municipal building and safety” umbrella, meaning you should expect permits for key waterproofing activities—especially where you are altering structural components or changing drainage connections. In practice, foundation excavation, structural crack repair, and changes to lot drainage (for example, work that affects how runoff is redirected) typically require a permit. If your plan includes connecting a sump discharge to storm or sanitary services, you generally need municipal approval before any tie-in.

For structural crack repair—particularly horizontal cracks in block walls or major step cracks—homeowners should plan on a structural engineer’s assessment to confirm whether underpinning or other structural remedies are needed. That matters because waterproofing products won’t “fix” movement; they only address water.

To verify a contractor in Logan Lake, start by checking the correct licence status through the provincial licensing registry for the trade(s) they perform. Then request a certificate of liability insurance naming you as the interested party (or showing appropriate coverage limits). Ask for proof of WSIB/WCB coverage. For clearance, you should be able to receive a clearance letter or equivalent documentation from the contractor’s insurance/workplace account. Finally, if structural repairs are contemplated, confirm they have documented engineering support (either in-house or via a partner engineer).

Interior vs exterior waterproofing — what does Logan Lake need?

Interior and exterior waterproofing are designed for different realities. Exterior waterproofing means full excavation along the foundation, installation of a new membrane, new drainage tile/weeping tile, proper backfill, and reinstatement. It addresses the source of water entry and reduces hydrostatic pressure, but it’s significantly more expensive and disruptive because you’re removing landscaping and sometimes concrete to access the foundation.

Interior waterproofing—typically a perimeter drain channel, sump pit, and sump pump—captures water after it has entered and routes it away from your foundation. It’s less invasive and often the practical choice when excavation access is difficult. However, interior systems do not stop water pressure from building against the wall itself. In Thompson–Okanagan conditions, that matters because freeze-thaw can widen existing cracks, turning small seepage into recurring wet seasons.

For Logan Lake, the right choice depends on foundation type and how the site holds water. Poured concrete walls often respond well to crack injection when the crack type is suitable (dry/structurally bonded cracks for epoxy; active leaks for polyurethane) and when drainage is managed. Block foundations frequently benefit from interior drainage as a complement, since block joints and mortar bedding can be harder to fully seal from the outside depending on excavation access. Because spring in British Columbia can bring power interruptions during high-usage periods, sump pump projects often justify a backup system (battery or water-powered) so the system can keep running if the power goes out.

A typical justification example: an exterior project can sit in the $18,000–$40,000 range, while a well-installed interior perimeter drain and sump often falls in $9,000–$22,000. If your soil holds water (clay pockets/silt) and the weeping tile is failing, the exterior approach may prevent repeated crack “re-sealing” and protect the long-term condition of the walls—making the higher price worth it.

How to choose a waterproofing contractor in Logan Lake

Choosing the right contractor in Logan Lake comes down to verification and scope clarity. First, confirm the contractor is properly licensed in British Columbia for the work they perform, and request their certificate of liability insurance so you know you’re covered if there’s damage to your home during excavation or concrete work. Next, ask for proof of WSIB/WCB coverage. You can verify by checking the contractor’s online standing (where available), and by requesting documentation directly—then match the dates and coverage details to the project period.

Get 2–3 itemised written quotes rather than a lump-sum: you want a labour and materials breakdown showing what’s included (excavation length, membrane type, drainage pipe size, pumping components, disposal, and reinstatement). Read the exclusions carefully—many disputes come from not understanding whether permit pulling, saw-cutting, concrete removal, or backfill compaction testing are included.

For warranty, ask for two parts: the workmanship warranty length (and whether it’s transferable if you sell) and the product/manufacturer warranty for membranes, drainage components, and sealants. Ask how issues are handled if there’s a repeat leak after a spring freeze-thaw cycle.

On payment, keep it conservative: never pay more than 10–15% upfront, and hold back a portion until the job is complete and cleaned up. Finally, insist on a start date and completion estimate in writing so seasonal scheduling in the Thompson–Okanagan doesn’t turn into months of uncertainty.

  • Ask what site investigation they do first (moisture mapping, inspection of old weeping tile, crack profiling).
  • Confirm the discharge location for sump water (clear daylight route or approved connection).
  • Get confirmation on whether they include engineering support when cracks suggest movement.
  • Require an itemised scope: excavation length/height, membrane system, pipe size, slope, and discharge details.
  • Ask what they do about mould/efflorescence before sealing.
  • Check that disposal and backfill compaction are included (not “allowance only”).
  • Clarify reinstatement: topsoil, sod, gravel, concrete/asphalt patches, and window well framing.
  • Request the exact warranty terms in writing (workmanship vs product).
  • Confirm pump specs if included (horsepower/flow rate) and the backup plan (battery or water-powered).
  • Verify permit responsibility: who pulls the permit and what’s the lead time?
  • Use a payment schedule you can live with: small deposit, holdback, no large milestone payments.
  • Ask for a moisture plan after completion (drying time, monitoring, and documentation).

Red flags in Logan Lake: a quote that’s only a “seal everything” pitch with no crack type assessment; no mention of backup power when recommending sump pumps; vague wording like “as required” without allowances for excavation/reinstatement; refusing to provide insurance/clearance or not showing WSIB/WCB; and warranties that don’t clearly separate workmanship from product coverage.

Do I need a permit for foundation work in British Columbia?

In British Columbia, you should expect permits for most foundation excavation and structural-affecting work. Typically, foundation excavation, structural crack repair, and changes to lot drainage require a permit, and any connection of sump discharge to municipal services usually needs municipal approval. For Logan Lake homeowners, the practical takeaway is to ask your contractor to confirm what they will pull (and who is responsible) before any excavation begins. If the cracks look structural—such as major step cracking or horizontal cracking—an engineer’s assessment is commonly required to determine whether underpinning is needed, which also impacts permit scope. Always ask for the permit number and inspection milestones in writing.

How long does waterproofing last?

How long waterproofing lasts in Logan Lake depends on whether the job addresses the source of water entry, not just the symptoms. Exterior systems that restore membrane and drainage typically provide the longest protection, especially on older homes where weeping tile and drainage paths fail over time. Interior systems can last many years too, but they manage water after it enters; if hydrostatic pressure remains high, cracks may keep opening and require follow-up crack sealing. With the Thompson–Okanagan freeze-thaw cycle, even small gaps can worsen if drainage isn’t correct. Homeowners often choose exterior work when the soil holds moisture, where pricing commonly sits around $18,000–$40,000. Interior perimeter drainage projects often fall in $9,000–$22,000 and can perform very well when designed and maintained appropriately.

Can I waterproof my basement from the inside only?

Yes, in many Logan Lake cases you can waterproof from the inside only—especially when exterior excavation isn’t feasible due to tight access, mature landscaping, or when the goal is to control water intrusion. Interior perimeter drains, a sump pit, and a sump pump system can capture seepage along slab edges and redirect it away from your foundation. However, interior work generally doesn’t stop hydrostatic pressure against the wall itself. In Thompson–Okanagan sites with clay pockets or dense silt, water pressure can keep driving seepage through joints and cracks, meaning you may still need crack injection and possibly exterior drainage later. If you’re comparing approaches, interior solutions commonly land around $9,000–$22,000, while exterior excavation typically sits higher (often $18,000–$40,000) because it targets the source.

What causes foundation cracks in Logan Lake?

In Logan Lake and the broader Thompson–Okanagan, common causes include freeze-thaw movement and soil expansion, plus aging foundations and drainage problems. Repeated freeze-thaw can widen existing hairline cracks and joints, and where backfill or the surrounding soil retains moisture (for example, clay pockets), lateral pressure can increase against foundation walls. Older housing stock is also a factor: many homes built before 1981 are more likely to have dated waterproofing assemblies and drainage patterns, which can allow more water to contact and stress the foundation over time. Poor lot grading and failing downspouts or weeping tile can also increase the amount of water entering around the foundation perimeter, which worsens movement. If cracks are horizontal or show signs of structural movement, you should request engineering input before relying on injection alone.

How long does basement waterproofing take in Logan Lake?

Timelines vary by scope, foundation type, access, and whether exterior excavation is required. In general, small interior crack injection jobs can be completed relatively quickly (often within days, depending on preparation and number of ports), while interior perimeter drainage and sump installations usually take longer due to saw-cutting, installing drains, plumbing/electrical, and drying time. Exterior waterproofing can take weeks because excavation, membrane installation, drainage tile placement, backfill compaction, and full reinstatement (landscaping, concrete/asphalt repairs) are all sequential steps. In British Columbia’s Thompson–Okanagan climate, scheduling also matters because spring melt and heavy rain can slow progress and affect backfill and curing.
