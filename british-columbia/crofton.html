Permits & regulations in British Columbia

In British Columbia, foundation excavation, structural crack repair, and changes to lot drainage typically require a building permit. If your waterproofing plan involves significant structural work—such as addressing horizontal movement in a block wall, step cracking that suggests settlement, or underpinning—expect permit and documentation requirements. Sump pump installations can also trigger approval requirements if you’re connecting discharge to a municipal system (storm or sanitary), or if the discharge method changes how water leaves your property.

For structural crack repair, a structural engineer’s assessment is often required for major cracks (especially horizontal or clearly progressive movement). When that’s the case, underpinning or localized foundation stabilization may be recommended—so a waterproofing contractor should provide evidence of engineering support and coordinate scope with the proper professionals where needed. Also confirm they carry liability insurance and WSIB/WCB coverage, even for residential work.

How to verify as a Crofton homeowner (step-by-step):

  • Check the contractor’s business credentials and licence status through the appropriate online BC registry/resource and confirm they match the exact scope (foundation drainage/waterproofing, excavation, structural crack repair).
  • Request a current certificate of insurance (liability) and confirm effective dates; don’t accept expired coverage.
  • Ask for WSIB/WCB clearance or proof of coverage for their workers; verify it’s current for the start date.
  • For any structural crack repair, ask whether an engineer is involved and request the engineer’s letter or assessment scope (not just a verbal claim).
  • If the contractor pulls permits, confirm in writing whether the permit is included in the quote.

Interior vs exterior waterproofing — what does Crofton need?

The fundamental difference is that exterior waterproofing permanently addresses the source of water entry by excavating around the foundation, installing a new exterior membrane, restoring proper drainage tile and discharge, and then re-backfilling. Interior waterproofing manages water after it enters by collecting seepage near the foundation with a perimeter drain channel, sump pit, and sump pump system. In Crofton’s Lower Mainland–Southwest climate, where persistent rainfall can maintain hydrostatic pressure, exterior systems are the most direct way to reduce the pressure pushing against walls and slab edges—but they cost more due to excavation, access work, and landscaping restoration.

Interior solutions are often the better fit when excavation is impractical (finished landscaping, decks hard up against foundation, or limited access), and they can still be highly effective when installed correctly and paired with adequate sump capacity. Poured concrete walls often seal well with crack injection when the wall is stable; block foundations frequently benefit from interior drainage as a practical complement because moisture paths can be harder to fully stop from the inside alone. In BC’s wet shoulder seasons, having a backup system on your sump pump matters—during high rainfall or spring events, power interruptions can happen, and interior systems rely on the pump to keep the dry zone dry.

For a concrete example: if your issue is localized wall seepage with a small number of cracks, crack injection can sometimes remain near the $500–$2,000 foundation crack repair band. But if you’re seeing recurring water at the perimeter and your weeping tile is failed, interior waterproofing typically aligns with the $8,000–$18,000 band; the cost is justified because it adds drainage capture and sump control rather than only sealing symptoms. Exterior excavation—usually chosen when you need to reduce hydrostatic pressure at the source—tends to sit in the $15,000–$30,000 band.

How to choose a waterproofing contractor in Crofton

How does Crofton's soil affect my foundation?

Crofton’s waterfront influence and coastal conditions mean groundwater and saturated backfill are often the real problem, not just seasonal ground movement. In the Lower Mainland–Southwest, persistent rainfall can keep the soil around your foundation saturated, increasing hydrostatic pressure against basement walls and slab edges. When original perimeter drainage (like older weeping tile) fails, water has fewer paths to escape, so seepage can show up during long wet stretches.

Soil can also impact how well drainage tile and backfill perform after excavation. If the area around the footing is more poorly draining, sump run times can increase and you may need a more complete interior system rather than a minimal repair. Do I need a permit for foundation work in British Columbia?

In British Columbia, foundation excavation and changes to lot drainage typically require a building permit. Sump pump discharge connections can also trigger municipal approval if you’re altering where water goes.

What often does not require a permit is limited, cosmetic patching after a waterproofing repair, or minor drainage tweaks like simple downspout extensions—though always confirm based on the scope of your contractor’s proposal. How long does waterproofing last?

It depends on the method and whether you addressed the water entry source. Exterior waterproofing—full excavation plus membrane and a correctly functioning drainage tile system—typically offers the most durable results, often 20+ years when installed to standard and maintained. Interior systems also last long, but they manage water after it enters, so their performance depends on sump capacity, discharge routing, and whether the pump is maintained through wet seasons.

Targeted crack injection can last 10–20 years when matched to the crack type (structural/epoxy for stable cracks, polyurethane for active seepage). For budgeting, it helps to compare typical scopes: a small crack repair might sit around the $500–$2,000 band, while a full interior perimeter drain and sump system is often closer to $8,000–$18,000. Lower cost doesn’t always mean shorter life—what matters is the match between the cause and the fix.

Can I waterproof my basement from the inside only?

Yes—many Crofton basements can be effectively managed from the inside only, especially when exterior access is limited by landscaping, decks, or nearby hardscapes. An interior perimeter drain channel and sump pit can capture seepage and reduce dampness, which is often the practical solution when water is already penetrating and you want less disruption. In wet Lower Mainland–Southwest weather, though, you need to size the sump and discharge properly because groundwater pressure can be sustained during prolonged rain.

Interior-only work usually doesn’t “stop” hydrostatic pressure at the wall; it controls the water where it collects. If your perimeter weeping tile is completely failed and you’re dealing with persistent saturation, you may still get better long-term results with exterior excavation and drainage replacement—often within the $15,000–$30,000 exterior band.

What causes foundation cracks in Crofton?

Foundation cracks in Crofton are commonly driven by moisture and freeze-thaw cycles. When the surrounding soil stays saturated for long periods, water can enter through joints and hairline cracks. During colder periods, freeze-thaw can widen those openings and increase water movement, accelerating deterioration—especially around corners, penetrations, and wall-to-slab transitions.

In older homes, the likelihood of failed drainage rises because original perimeter drainage and waterproofing materials may be long past their service life. If water is constantly present at the wall, the crack can become “active,” producing seepage during storms. That changes the waterproofing approach—polyurethane injection is often used for active leak conditions, and interior drainage may be needed alongside crack sealing. Budget expectations often range from $500–$2,000 for isolated crack repair up to a full drainage-and-sump scope if leakage is widespread.

The biggest quote differences in Crofton usually come from site access constraints, how thoroughly the perimeter drainage is addressed, and whether the plan includes sump pump redundancy (like a backup system).

Next, compare warranties: workmanship coverage length, product/manufacturer warranty terms, and whether the warranty transfers. Confirm the payment schedule is reasonable—never more than about 10–15% upfront. If one quote sits closer to the $8,000–$18,000 interior range and another proposes exterior excavation closer to $15,000–$30,000, ask what evidence they used (e.g., drainage condition, crack type, evidence of active seepage) to justify the method.
