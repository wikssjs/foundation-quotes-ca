Permits & regulations in British Columbia

In British Columbia, many basement waterproofing-related tasks require careful compliance because you’re often changing drainage patterns or addressing items that may affect structural safety. Foundation excavation and most changes to lot drainage typically require a building permit. Structural crack repair—especially horizontal cracks in block walls or any signs of significant wall movement—often needs an assessment by a structural engineer to determine whether underpinning or other structural work is required. If you’re installing or modifying sump discharge so it connects to municipal storm or sanitary services, municipal approval is commonly needed before any tie-in is made.

Work that typically does not require a permit in many cases includes localized interior sealing/injection of non-structural cracks (when there’s no structural movement), small interior drainage repairs that don’t alter structural elements, and routine downspout extensions that stay within common surface drainage practices. However, because exact permit triggers vary by scope and connection method, always confirm with your contractor and your local permitting requirements before starting.

How to verify a contractor for Whonnock/B.C. projects, step by step: (1) ask for their B.C. licence details (and the business info they operate under) and check it through the relevant provincial registry; (2) request a current certificate of insurance showing liability coverage—make sure the jobsite address is covered; (3) request WSIB/WCB clearance/coverage documentation if they employ workers (or provide documentation of coverage as applicable); (4) for structural work, confirm they have engineering support in place and will provide stamped documentation when needed; and (5) keep copies of everything with your quote package.

Interior vs exterior waterproofing — what does Whonnock need?

In Whonnock, the core difference is whether you stop the water at its source or you manage it after it enters. Exterior waterproofing involves full excavation, new membrane, new drainage tile (weeping tile), and proper backfill and re-grading. Done correctly, it directly reduces hydrostatic pressure against the foundation wall—often the most complete solution—but it’s also the most disruptive and most expensive due to excavation labour and yard restoration. Interior waterproofing usually means an interior perimeter drain channel, a sump pit, and a sump pump to collect seepage and keep the basement drier. It typically costs less and has less landscape disruption, but it doesn’t stop water from reaching the wall; instead, it intercepts water after entry.

Given Lower Mainland–Southwest conditions—high groundwater pressure during prolonged rainfall, plus freeze-thaw that aggravates cracks—many Whonnock basements benefit from interior drainage when exterior access is constrained (existing landscaping, decks, driveways, or limited side yards). Poured concrete walls often respond well to crack injection when the issue is localized, but block walls frequently need interior drainage as a practical complement because water pathways can be harder to seal completely from the inside alone. In a spring wet stretch, power reliability matters: a battery backup or water-powered backup sump can keep the system running if an outage coincides with high groundwater.

Price-wise, it can be justified to spend more for exterior work when you’re replacing a failed perimeter system end-to-end. For example, if you’re seeing repeated seepage and the original weeping tile is collapsed, exterior scope often lands in the $15,000–$30,000 range. If you can’t excavate fully or the leak is mainly localized to the perimeter at the inside footing, an interior perimeter drain and sump frequently fits into the $8,000–$18,000 band—especially when paired with targeted crack injection where appropriate.

How to choose a waterproofing contractor in Whonnock

To choose a waterproofing contractor in Whonnock, start with proof they’re properly authorized and insured. In British Columbia, ask for their business licence information and current liability insurance certificate—confirm it’s active for the period of your job and that they list you and/or the jobsite as applicable per standard practice. Next, verify WSIB/WCB coverage for their workers: request documentation such as a clearance letter (or current proof of coverage) and confirm the names under the coverage match the contracting entity performing the work. A legitimate contractor should provide these documents without pressure and before any work begins.

Then get 2–3 itemised written quotes. You want labour and materials broken down—examples include excavation, disposal, membrane/drain tile materials, pipe runs, pump selection, electrical/discharge scope, and concrete re-pour or floor reinstatement. Make sure the scope clearly states what’s excluded (for instance: mould remediation, engineering fees, or landscaping reinstatement beyond standard backfill). Ask whether permits are included or billed separately, and whether disposal/haul-off is included.

Warranty matters: ask for the workmanship warranty term, the product/manufacturer warranty for membranes/drainage components, and whether warranties are transferable if you sell your home. For payment, never pay more than 10–15% upfront; hold back the balance until the work passes final review. Finally, require a written timeline with an estimated start date and completion date so you can plan around weather-dependent excavation and curing.

What is a battery backup sump pump and do I need one in Whonnock?

A battery backup sump pump is an emergency power system that keeps your sump running during power outages. In Whonnock, it’s especially valuable during spring wet periods when groundwater loads are high and a few hours without pumping can raise water levels quickly. Battery systems typically monitor pump operation and automatically switch over when grid power drops. Whether you “need” one depends on your risk: if you’ve had basement water during outages, if you’re installing a new sump as part of an interior drainage upgrade, or if your home sits on a tighter drainage lot where water has less time to drain away, backup is often a wise addition. Many installations pair the sump system with backup and land in the $1,000–$5,000 range depending on the setup.

Interior vs exterior waterproofing — which is better?

“Better” depends on where the water problem originates. Exterior waterproofing is typically the most complete fix because it addresses water entry at the foundation exterior by rebuilding perimeter drainage and installing membrane—however, it’s more disruptive and usually costs more, often in the $15,000–$30,000 range in the Lower Mainland–Southwest. Interior waterproofing (French drain channel + sump) manages water after it enters. It’s less invasive, commonly landing around $8,000–$18,000, and is a strong option when excavation access is limited. In Whonnock’s wet, mild climate with freeze-thaw cycles, we often combine interior drainage with targeted crack injection for best results when source control isn’t fully practical. Why is my basement leaking in Whonnock?

Basement leaks in Whonnock usually come from water pressure and failed drainage pathways. Most commonly, heavy rainfall saturates soil and raises groundwater levels, then water seeks the path of least resistance through foundation joints, cracks, or around penetrations. Freeze-thaw can widen small cracks and allow seepage to start or increase during spring. If your home’s original weeping tile is undersized, disconnected, or has failed over time, hydrostatic pressure has nowhere to go. Window wells and downspouts that discharge too close to the foundation can add surface water to the same problem. Finally, foundation material matters: block walls often create more internal pathways, while poured concrete may respond better to crack injection when cracks are stable. A proper diagnosis is what prevents repeat leaks.
