Basement waterproofing options and costs in McMillan

In McMillan, British Columbia, basement waterproofing typically comes down to two strategies: stop water at the foundation from the outside, or manage it once it gets inside. With McMillan sitting in the Lower Mainland–Southwest region, homeowners often face persistent groundwater pressure during our wet, mild winters—especially in communities where the housing stock is aging. In McMillan’s population of 9,250 people (Statistics Canada, 2021 Census), many properties are older enough that the original drainage systems (weeping tile) have likely deteriorated. When drainage fails, hydrostatic pressure finds the path of least resistance through block or poured-concrete joints, cracks, and cold seams.

Lower Mainland–Southwest pricing is shaped by soil saturation and water-table conditions that keep seepage risk active year-round, not just during spring melt. Freeze-thaw cycles can widen existing cracks and joints, while labour and access constraints on tight lots push exterior excavation and mechanical breaking higher. This is especially true around older neighbourhood pockets near commercial corridors and established residential streets, where machine access is limited and crews must protect landscaping and hardscape.

That’s why it’s common to see contractors quote the same “wet basement” problem at different price levels—one job may replace the source drainage system, while another focuses on interior capture with sump pump upgrades. What affects the price of waterproofing in McMillan

In the Lower Mainland–Southwest region, it’s not unusual to see quotes for the same basement-water complaint vary by 30–50%. The difference usually isn’t “who’s cheaper”—it’s what each contractor is actually solving. Exterior work can fall into the higher end because excavation and mechanical breaking are labour-intensive, especially on established lots where access is tight. Interior systems are typically less disruptive, but if the source drainage and grading aren’t addressed, interior-only solutions may cost less initially while still requiring additional upgrades later.

Three drivers separate regional costs from the national average: soil type, water table, and freeze-thaw. First, coastal BC soils tend to be more about persistent saturation than extreme expansion. Still, if your backfill or native material holds water, drainage can stay overwhelmed and seepage continues. Second, higher water tables raise sump run times and can demand larger or better-performing pumping and discharge systems. Third, freeze-thaw widens cracks and joint openings, allowing water to penetrate and accelerating deterioration.

Concrete examples from McMillan projects I’ve seen: (1) a basement with failing perimeter drainage often requires exterior excavation simply to restore proper weeping tile routing; without that, an interior perimeter drain may saturate the channel more frequently, pushing costs toward a full interior retrofit plus pump capacity upgrades. (2) Block foundations with horizontal movement cracks often need crack injection plus interior capture, because sealing alone may not keep up with hydrostatic pressure. (3) Homes with long, difficult crack lines combined with older membrane systems typically land in the mid-range crack repair costs up to the higher band once combined with sump upgrades—sometimes moving a job from a basic $8,000–$18,000 interior target toward a larger scope.

Permits & regulations in British Columbia

In British Columbia, foundation excavation, structural crack repair, and changes to lot drainage typically require a building permit. Sump pump installations that connect discharge to municipal systems (storm or sanitary) usually need municipal approval, because the receiving system and flow control matter. For structural crack repair—especially horizontal cracks in block walls or major step cracking—an assessment by a structural engineer is often required to confirm whether the wall needs underpinning or other structural remediation before waterproofing can be considered complete.

Before you sign a contract in McMillan, verify the contractor’s qualifications step-by-step:

1) Licensing/registration: ask for the contractor’s BC registry details (and confirm what scope they’re authorized to perform). If they cannot provide clear proof of authorization for the work they’re proposing, treat it as a red flag. 2) Liability insurance: request a current certificate of insurance showing the project address and coverage limits; ensure the certificate matches the company named on the quote. 3) WSIB/WCB coverage: in BC, confirm the coverage and clearance. Ask for the clearance letter (or proof) and check the employer name matches the quoting entity. 4) Engineering support: for structural crack work, ask whether they include engineer assessment or coordinate directly with one.

Finally, confirm whether the permit pull is included in the quote and who is responsible for inspections if required—don’t assume it’s automatic.

Interior vs exterior waterproofing — what does McMillan need?

For McMillan basements, the key difference is source control versus water management. Exterior waterproofing involves full excavation, installing a new membrane, restoring or replacing perimeter drainage tile, and then carefully backfilling. It directly addresses where water is entering by relieving hydrostatic pressure against the wall. The trade-off is cost and disruption: excavation on tight Lower Mainland lots often means more labour, mechanical breaking where rock or compacted fill exists, and greater landscaping restoration.

Interior waterproofing—such as a perimeter drain channel, sump pit, and sump pump—captures water after it enters and reduces standing moisture inside. It’s usually less invasive and can be the best practical choice when excavation is constrained, when the primary issue is seepage along slab edges, or when you’re pairing drainage capture with crack sealing. But interior systems do not stop hydrostatic pressure on the foundation itself; that’s why, in persistent high-water situations common in coastal BC, an effective plan often pairs interior capture with crack repair and a reliable pumping system.

In McMillan, poured concrete walls generally respond well to crack injection, but block foundations frequently need a complementary approach: interior drainage to manage ongoing seepage plus injection where cracks are clearly identified. Also, because spring storms and power interruptions can happen, sump pump backup systems matter—at minimum, battery backup helps prevent overflow during outages while you wait for power restoration.

Example: if your basement shows active seepage and failing perimeter drainage, a full exterior system can land around $15,000–$30,000. If you instead install interior capture and a properly sized sump with backup, you may be closer to $8,000–$18,000—but only do the interior-first approach if surface grading, downspouts, and drainage capacity are addressed, and the leaks can be consistently captured.

Frequently asked questions — waterproofing in McMillan

How long does waterproofing last?

In McMillan and across the Lower Mainland–Southwest, longevity depends on whether the work controls water at the source or only manages it indoors. Exterior systems that restore drainage tile routing and apply a quality membrane—then backfill correctly—often perform for 20+ years when installed to suit local saturation conditions. Interior systems typically last 10–15 years or more, but performance relies heavily on sump pump reliability, float switches, discharge piping, and whether you’ve addressed the ongoing water load from grading and downspouts. For crack repairs, injection can be long-lasting, but freeze-thaw can widen untreated joints again if drainage remains overwhelmed. If your weeping tile is failing, you’ll usually see recurring symptoms unless drainage is corrected.

Can I waterproof my basement from the inside only?

Yes, many McMillan homeowners do interior-only waterproofing, particularly when excavation isn’t practical due to tight lot access or when the main issue is seepage capture along slab edges. Interior solutions usually involve a perimeter drain channel, sump pit, and sump pump, with the goal of collecting infiltrating water and keeping the basement dry. However, interior work doesn’t relieve hydrostatic pressure against the wall—so if groundwater is high and persistent (a common coastal BC reality), you may need ongoing capture and a reliable pumping system, often with battery backup. Interior waterproofing often falls into the $8,000–$18,000 band, while exterior excavation commonly sits higher at $15,000–$30,000. The best inside-only plan still includes grading and downspout corrections to reduce the amount of water reaching the foundation.

What causes foundation cracks in McMillan?

Foundation cracks in McMillan typically come from a combination of water and movement. In coastal BC, wet conditions keep soil saturated, and freeze-thaw cycles widen existing cracks and joints over time. That repeated opening and closing can lead to fresh seepage points, efflorescence, and moisture along the floor/wall junction. If the foundation is block or if the basement has older drainage systems, inadequate weeping tile performance can raise hydrostatic pressure, increasing the risk of hairline cracking turning into more noticeable gaps. Step cracks can also indicate differential movement, which is why major structural cracking may require a structural engineer assessment. Even when the crack is repaired, the water pathway must be addressed—otherwise, the crack can re-open under ongoing pressure.

How do I compare waterproofing quotes?

To compare quotes fairly in McMillan, insist on scope clarity. Ask each contractor for an itemised breakdown: what they’re installing (membrane type, drainage tile layout, sump pit components), how they’ll prepare surfaces, disposal and disposal fees, saw-cut limits, and what finishing work is included. Confirm whether permit pull is included and who handles approvals if the sump discharge needs municipal authorization. Look for specific details on drainage capacity and pumping: sump pump model/specs, check valves, and whether battery backup is part of the recommended design. Compare timelines, warranty (workmanship length plus product warranty), and payment schedules. If one quote is far below $15,000–$30,000 for exterior work but includes no drainage tile replacement, it may be under-solving the source problem.

How long does basement waterproofing take in McMillan?

Project length varies with access, excavation requirements, and the extent of repairs. A typical interior waterproofing scope (perimeter drain channel, sump pit, and pump install) often takes several days to a couple of weeks depending on concrete removal, curing times for crack repair, and any mould/efflorescence remediation. Exterior waterproofing is usually longer because excavation and mechanical breaking can be time-consuming on established lots, and because restoring landscaping and grading adds days. Weather can influence scheduling in Lower Mainland rainy periods, especially if the site needs to be kept workable for backfilling and membrane installation. If structural cracking requires engineering assessment, that can add lead time before work starts. Your contractor should provide a written start date and completion estimate that includes weather contingency.

What is a weeping tile and does my McMillan home have one?

A weeping tile is the underground perimeter drain system (often a historical tile/pipe drain) that collects groundwater around the foundation and directs it to a sump or discharge route. Many older homes in the Lower Mainland–Southwest were built with some version of perimeter drainage, but it can fail over time due to corrosion, settling, blockages, or improper outlet connections. Whether your McMillan home has one depends on your construction era, renovation history, and the site’s original drainage layout. You usually can’t confirm it visually without inspection or excavation, but clues include basement water patterns, the presence/condition of a sump pit, and what drainage was documented in prior renovations. If you’re dealing with frequent seepage, it’s a good idea to ask contractors to evaluate the drainage system condition before choosing between an interior-only plan and exterior replacement.
