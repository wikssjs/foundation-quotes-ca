Third, older housing stock in Calgary’s established neighbourhoods often has aged or clogged weeping tile, so seepage and localized wall leaks become frequent failure points rather than one isolated problem.

Two concrete examples we see in Glamorgan: (1) homes with exterior weeping tile that has collapsed under saturated clay often require exterior excavation to re-establish a functioning perimeter system; that’s a cost jump into the exterior band of roughly $9,000–$25,000. Permits & regulations in Alberta

In Alberta, some basement and drainage-related work requires permits, particularly when you’re altering drainage patterns, touching structural elements, or changing how water is managed around the foundation. Typical permit-requiring items include foundation excavation (when it changes foundation exposure and grading near the building), structural crack repair when it involves addressing major foundation movement (for example, horizontal cracks in block walls or other signs that may affect stability), and lot drainage changes that meaningfully alter how water flows away from the house. Also, sump pump installations that connect to the storm or sanitary sewer generally require municipal approval before discharge is made.

For homeowners, the safest approach is to treat “structural crack repair” and “new discharge routing” as permit/approval territory, not just a cosmetic repair. When a crack is suspected to be structural, a structural engineer’s assessment is often required to confirm whether underpinning or additional structural work is needed—especially when cracking pattern suggests wall movement rather than simple shrinkage.

How to verify contractor compliance in Glamorgan, step-by-step: (1) Ask for the contractor’s Alberta licence details (and confirm they match the scope of the work they’re proposing). (2) Request a certificate of insurance showing liability coverage; verify it covers the job site activities (excavation, saw-cutting, electrical for sump, etc.). (3) Confirm WSIB/WCB coverage—ask for clearance or confirmation documentation and keep copies for your records. (4) For structural crack work, ask whether they coordinate engineering and provide documentation for any engineered recommendations.

Interior vs exterior waterproofing — what does Glamorgan need?

For Glamorgan homes, the choice between interior and exterior waterproofing comes down to whether you want to address water at the source or control it after it enters. Exterior waterproofing involves full excavation, new wall membrane, and perimeter drainage tile—then careful backfill. Done correctly, it’s the most permanent way to stop water migration into foundation walls. The trade-off is disruption: removing landscaping, managing heavy clay spoils, and restoring patios/grass/driveways all add time and cost, which is why exterior projects typically start around the $9,000–$25,000 band.

Interior waterproofing—typically a perimeter drain channel, sump pit, and sump pump—intercepts seepage at the slab or wall junction after water penetrates. It’s usually less invasive and can fit many finished-basement homes, with typical interior scopes often landing in the $5,000–$15,000 range depending on complexity. However, interior systems don’t relieve hydrostatic pressure on the wall in the same direct way as exterior membrane and drainage; they manage that pressure through dependable pumping and drainage.

Given southern Alberta’s freeze–thaw cycles and clay-heavy soils, many Glamorgan basements benefit from interior solutions when exterior access is limited, but they need strong sump design, including backup. Spring flooding and short outages during storms mean a primary pump without backup can still lead to localized flooding. On poured concrete walls, crack injection (when cracks are stable) can help keep interior systems effective; on block foundations, interior drainage often becomes a practical complement because joints and mortar pathways can persist even after localized repairs.

Example of when the price difference is justified: if you already have to remove and replace a patio and landscaping anyway, an exterior membrane and drainage tile approach can be worth selecting because it targets the source, whereas repeated interior patching for the same wet wall often costs more over several seasons.

How to choose a waterproofing contractor in Glamorgan

Start with compliance and proof, not promises. Ask for documentation of Alberta licensing for the work they’re claiming to do, then request a certificate of insurance showing liability coverage for the project scope (excavation, demolition, electrical for pumps, and any work near foundations). For WSIB/WCB, ask for clearance/confirmation documentation—don’t rely on verbal assurances. A reputable Glamorgan contractor will hand you these documents quickly because it reduces their risk and yours.

Next, get 2–3 itemised, written quotes—not one-page summaries. You want a labour + materials breakdown with clear inclusions such as membrane type, drainage tile size, discharge routing, sump controls, and patch/restoration responsibilities. Verify whether the quote includes permit pull (when needed), disposal fees for heavy clay spoils, and restoration like floor patching, grading, and re-seeding.

Warranty matters in waterproofing: ask for (1) workmanship warranty length and terms, (2) product/manufacturer warranty details, and (3) whether warranties are transferable if you sell the home. Payment schedules should be conservative—never more than about 10–15% upfront. Hold back a portion until the job is complete and you’ve confirmed operational testing of pumps and drainage.

Finally, insist on a timeline: a written start date, estimated completion window, and how they handle weather delays typical of Calgary spring freeze–thaw. Good waterproofing teams plan for short working windows and manage backfill timing correctly.

Interior vs exterior waterproofing — which is better?

Neither option is universally “better”—it depends on the source of water entry and your foundation details in Glamorgan. Exterior waterproofing (excavation, membrane, drainage tile) addresses the source and is most reliable in clay soils that hold water and expand during freeze–thaw. Why is my basement leaking in Glamorgan?

Most leaks in Glamorgan are caused by water migration through cracks and joints combined with freeze–thaw widening those pathways. Clay and clay-till soils can saturate and increase lateral pressure on foundation walls, which worsens cracking over time. If your home has older weeping tile, it may be clogged, disconnected, or collapsed—so water can build up and seep into poured concrete or block walls and the slab edge. Another common cause is surface runoff: downspouts, grading, or patio drainage directing water toward the foundation. In some low-lying pockets and during heavy spring storms, hydrostatic pressure can increase significantly, making sump management essential even when a homeowner has already applied sealants.

How do I know if a foundation crack is serious?

In Alberta, crack seriousness depends on crack type, pattern, and whether it shows signs of movement. Hairline shrinkage cracks in poured concrete can sometimes be addressed with injection if the crack is stable and dry enough for the correct product. However, horizontal cracks in block walls or cracks that widen across seasons, show steps across block joints, or appear with ongoing water seepage should be treated as potentially structural. In those cases, an engineer assessment is often recommended to confirm whether underpinning or other structural work is needed before sealing. A practical “field” indicator: if you see seepage, dampness, or efflorescence aligned with the crack during freeze–thaw and spring runoff, you likely have active water pathways that require drainage plus the right injection system.

How much does foundation crack repair cost in Glamorgan?

Foundation crack repair cost in Glamorgan typically depends on crack length, crack type, and whether the crack is actively leaking. For many localized repairs, homeowners commonly see pricing in the $500–$1,800 range for crack repair, with injection often being a key part of the solution. If the job requires more extensive drilling and multiple injection ports, or if remediation and testing are required before sealing, costs can land closer to the upper side of that band. If a crack is structural or needs engineered assessment, expect an additional step before any final sealing plan. Also remember: injection alone may not solve the issue if drainage and hydrostatic pressure remain unchecked—especially in clay soils and during Alberta spring melt.

Do I need a sump pump in Glamorgan?

You may need a sump pump in Glamorgan when your basement experiences hydrostatic pressure, seepage at the slab edge, or consistent water entry that a simple re-seal won’t stop. Interior drainage systems often include a sump basin so water intercepted by the perimeter drain can be pumped away reliably. Where seasonal groundwater and spring storms create pressure, sump pumps are particularly important for preventing overflow and reducing damage to drywall, flooring, and stored items. If you install a sump pump, consider a backup system (battery or water-powered) because power interruptions can occur during severe spring weather and storms. If your leak is minor and tied mainly to surface runoff, re-grading and downspout extensions may be enough—but if water shows up along multiple walls after melt or heavy rain, a sump typically becomes part of the long-term fix.
