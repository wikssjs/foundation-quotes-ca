Permits & regulations in Alberta

In Alberta, foundation excavation, structural crack repair, and changes to lot drainage typically require a building permit. If a sump pump is installed and the discharge is connected to a municipal sewer system (storm or sanitary), you usually need municipal approval for that tie-in. For structural crack repair—particularly horizontal cracks in block walls, significant step cracking, or signs that the foundation has moved—a structural engineer’s assessment is often required to determine whether underpinning or other structural work is needed before waterproofing proceeds.

For Stony Plain homeowners, the practical approach is to verify the contractor can legally and technically carry out the work:

Step 1: Confirm the contractor’s Alberta licence/registration where applicable by checking their business listing on the relevant provincial registry and comparing it to the legal name on the contract.

Step 2: Ask for a certificate of insurance (liability coverage) and ensure the policy is active for the project dates. Request WSIB/WCB clearance or coverage confirmation as applicable—your contractor should provide documentation, not just verbal assurance.

Step 3: For jobs involving engineering or major structural crack repair, ask whether engineering support is included, and whether the contractor will coordinate permits/inspections where required.

Step-by-step checks like this help protect you from workmanship gaps, failed permit assumptions, and delays caused by missing engineered scope.

Interior vs exterior waterproofing — what does Stony Plain need?

The fundamental difference is source control versus symptom control. Exterior waterproofing involves full excavation, a new membrane system, properly connected drainage tile, and backfill designed to keep water away from the foundation wall. It permanently addresses where water enters by relieving hydrostatic pressure—but it costs more and requires landscaping disruption. Interior waterproofing (a perimeter drain channel, sump pit, and pump system) manages water after it enters. It’s usually less invasive and often the best path when excavation is impractical, but it doesn’t stop pressure against the wall itself.

In Stony Plain, where clay-rich soils can stay saturated and Alberta’s freeze–thaw can widen cracks, exterior systems are often the “most complete” fix for recurring dampness, especially on walls that show consistent seepage after spring melt. That said, interior work is frequently justified for practical reasons. Poured concrete walls typically respond well to crack injection when cracks are stable; however, ongoing moisture may still require interior drainage to control seepage pathways. Block foundations, on the other hand, often benefit from an interior drain channel as a practical complement—particularly if mortar joints or capillary pathways contribute to dampness.

Alberta’s spring can also bring power instability risks during high water periods, so sump pump battery backup matters for peace of mind. If you’ve ever seen “water but no flood” during heavy rain, backup systems help prevent a small issue from becoming a full basement cleanup.

Dollar-wise, the exterior option can be justified. For example, a typical Stony Plain exterior excavation and drainage tile scope can land around $12,000–$25,000. If the same home only needs targeted interior collection and sump discharge, an interior perimeter drain plus sump system may fit closer to $8,000–$18,000—often a better value when you’re dealing with localized seepage and a stable foundation.
