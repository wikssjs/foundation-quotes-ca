Permits & regulations in Alberta

In Alberta, the work that affects structural integrity and changes how water leaves your lot usually triggers permit requirements. If they can’t provide documentation promptly, treat it as a red flag and move on.

Interior vs exterior waterproofing — what does Sexsmith need?

The fundamental difference in Sexsmith is whether you’re treating the source or managing the water after it enters. Exterior waterproofing—full excavation, new membrane, new drainage tile, and properly compacted backfill—addresses water at the foundation line and helps reduce hydrostatic pressure before it reaches your basement. It costs more and disrupts landscaping, but when done correctly it’s the most complete, long-lasting approach.

Interior waterproofing—perimeter drain channels, sump pits, and sump pumps—creates an interior drainage path and helps keep the basement dry by collecting and removing water once it gets in. In Alberta’s freeze-thaw climate, interior systems are often paired with crack management because cracks and joints widen each spring. That’s why, in Sexsmith basements, poured concrete walls frequently benefit from crack injection (after cleaning and prep), while block foundations commonly need interior drainage as a practical complement due to how moisture can travel through block and mortar joints.

Battery backup sump pumps are an important consideration in northern Alberta. During spring melt, power disruptions can happen, and if your sump loses power, groundwater can rise quickly. A backup system can prevent a small seep event from becoming a full overflow and cleanup.

Here’s where the price difference makes sense: if your inspection shows widespread exterior drainage failure, exterior work may fall in the $12,000–$25,000 band and be justified because it reduces recurrence. If your problem is localized to a few active cracks, staying closer to the $600–$2,500 crack-injection band (plus a modest interior system if needed) can be the smarter value—provided the cracks are correctly identified and the drainage plan accounts for clay-heavy, water-holding soil conditions.

Waterproofing, done properly, is built to manage water flow and pressure—often with a complete system such as exterior membrane plus drainage tile, or interior perimeter drains plus a sump pump that removes collected water.
