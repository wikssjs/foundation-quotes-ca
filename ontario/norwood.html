Permits & regulations in Ontario

In Ontario, foundation excavation and structural crack repair are commonly treated as regulated work, and homeowners are usually required to pull the appropriate permit(s) before work starts. As a practical rule of thumb for Norwood-area properties: if the work alters drainage for the foundation, involves repairs to load-bearing elements, or changes how water is managed around the house (especially tying new discharge routes into municipal systems), permits and approvals are typically required.

Work that DOES commonly require a permit includes: exterior foundation excavation and membrane work when it’s tied to foundation repairs, structural crack repairs where horizontal/step cracks in block walls suggest possible movement, and any changes to lot drainage that require engineering sign-off or municipal approval. Sump pump installations that connect to storm or sanitary sewer often need municipal approval. Work that typically does NOT require a permit includes: localized interior floor drain channel installation without altering discharge to municipal systems (confirm with the contractor and municipality), surface grading adjustments like downspout extensions (when kept within typical lot drainage practices), and isolated crack injection when it is limited to sealing of non-structural cracks—though structural assessment may still be needed if the crack pattern suggests movement.

Step-by-step, verify your contractor in Norwood: (1) check the online contractor registry for the applicable licence/registration (and any trade-specific credentials they claim); (2) request a current Certificate of Insurance showing liability coverage; (3) ask for WSIB/WCB clearance letters or proof of coverage; (4) for structural crack work, confirm they carry engineering support for assessment and any design recommendations. Then, confirm what permit(s) will be pulled and who is responsible.

Interior vs exterior waterproofing — what does Norwood need?

The fundamental difference is whether you stop water before it reaches the basement wall (exterior) or manage it after it enters (interior). Exterior waterproofing involves full excavation, new membrane, new drainage tile, and backfill. It’s the most permanent “source control” approach because it reduces hydrostatic pressure and directs groundwater away from foundation walls. The trade-off is cost and disruption—Norwood homes often have tight setbacks, decks, and established landscaping, so excavation and restoration can be expensive.

Interior waterproofing—perimeter drain channel, sump pit, and sump pump—does not stop water pressure on the wall itself. Instead, it captures seepage and relieves pressure from inside by intercepting water where it enters. This can be a strong choice in Norwood when the exterior access is limited, or when the leak pattern is consistent with localized entry points. Poured concrete walls often respond well to properly chosen crack injection (especially when cracks are stable), while block foundations frequently need interior drainage as a practical complement because water can travel through block joints and irregular mortar bedding even after targeted sealing.

Sump pump backup matters locally because spring storms and wet freeze–thaw conditions can drive sudden load spikes. A battery backup (or water-powered backup where appropriate) reduces the risk of overflow during short power interruptions.

Example: If you’re seeing a single active seepage crack plus dampness along one wall, targeted crack injection plus an interior sump upgrade might fall near $600–$3,000 for injection plus $800–$3,500 for pump equipment—often far cheaper than a full excavation. But if groundwater is saturating clay soils against your exterior and you have repeated water events, exterior work priced in the $7,000–$25,000 band can be justified because it replaces failing drainage and reduces the cause rather than only managing symptoms.

Payment schedules should protect you—never pay more than 10–15% upfront. Use a holdback until completion and walkthrough verification. Finally, ask for the start date and completion estimate in writing.

Frequently asked questions — waterproofing in Norwood

How does Norwood's soil affect my foundation?

Norwood’s surrounding Toronto area is known for mixed glacial till and pockets of dense clay. Clay holds water against foundation walls when saturated, which increases lateral pressure and makes seepage through tie holes, cold joints, and cracks more likely over repeated wet winters. When clay freezes and thaws, it can expand and widen small defects, turning hairline cracks into active leakage paths after a couple of seasons. If you also have older clay weeping tile or partially clogged drainage, the soil stays wet longer and sump pumps (if present) must run more often. That combination often shifts the right solution toward exterior source control when access allows, or interior drainage plus reliable pump backup when it doesn’t.

Do I need a permit for foundation work in Ontario?

In Ontario, permits are commonly required for foundation excavation and work that impacts drainage, structural elements, or discharge routes to municipal systems. For Norwood homeowners, the typical triggers are: exterior foundation excavation tied to membrane installation, structural crack repair when the crack pattern suggests movement, and sump pump discharge changes that connect to storm or sanitary services. Interior waterproofing can sometimes fall under work that’s permit-light, but it depends on whether you’re modifying discharge, altering load-bearing components, or changing lot drainage. The safest approach is to ask the contractor to list expected permits in the written scope, then confirm with the appropriate municipal process before work begins.

How long does waterproofing last?

Expected lifespan depends on the method and whether the underlying cause is solved. Exterior waterproofing with a new membrane and drainage tile typically provides the most durable outcome because it reduces the water entry and hydrostatic pressure at the foundation. Interior perimeter drainage and sump systems can last a long time, but longevity depends heavily on pump maintenance, discharge reliability, and whether the system is sized for wet-season loads. Crack injections can also last, but they only perform well if the crack is stable—epoxy is best suited to non-moving cracks, while polyurethane is used for active leaks. For planning, a common expectation is that a well-installed system supports many years, and upgrades like sump backup can help prevent freeze–thaw season failures that cause expensive interior damage.

Can I waterproof my basement from the inside only?

Yes, you can often waterproof from the inside only, and it’s a common Norwood choice when exterior access is difficult due to decks, driveways, or landscaping. Interior perimeter drain channels plus a sump can capture seepage and reduce basement dampness, especially when your main issue is water that has already entered through cracks or wall joints. However, interior-only work generally doesn’t stop hydrostatic pressure on the wall itself—so if your foundation is being actively pushed by saturated clay soils, you may still see recurring seepage until drainage and surface water control are corrected. A practical approach is interior drainage plus targeted crack repair, and adding a backup pump. If you’re dealing with severe lateral pressure, exterior excavation may be the more complete fix.

What causes foundation cracks in Norwood?

In Norwood, foundation cracks are often driven by soil moisture changes and freeze–thaw expansion. Dense clay soils swell when saturated and exert lateral pressure on foundation walls. As temperatures swing, frozen moisture expands and small voids in mortar or concrete can grow, widening hairline cracks over multiple winters. Older drainage systems that have partially failed—such as original weeping tile that has collapsed or clogged—keep soil wetter for longer periods, increasing movement and pressure. Also, settlement around additions or areas with different compaction can cause local stress that shows up as step or horizontal cracking, particularly in block foundation segments. A contractor should assess crack type and whether it’s active before choosing epoxy or polyurethane injection.

How do I compare waterproofing quotes?

Start by comparing itemized scopes, not just the total price. For Norwood projects, ask each contractor to break down labour and materials: excavation and membrane details (if exterior), pipe and tile layout, pump type, discharge routing, disposal, and what restoration is included. Confirm permit responsibilities in the written scope—especially for excavation, drainage changes, and sump discharge approvals. Ask about crack repair selection and diagnostics: epoxy versus polyurethane should match crack behavior. Also compare warranties: workmanship length and product/manufacturer coverage, and whether warranties are transferable. Finally, use price ranges as a check—many interior approaches fall in the $3,500–$15,000 band, while exterior work often lands in the $7,000–$25,000 band depending on access and drainage replacement.
